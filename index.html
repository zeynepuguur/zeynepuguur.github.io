01 · Moon Floral & Design Co.

Reducing uncertainty in a high-consideration service journey

Wedding florals are not a simple ecommerce purchase. Prospective clients arrive with partial information: a venue, a budget range, visual references, and an evolving idea of what they want. The research challenge was to understand what helped them judge fit and what interrupted the path to a useful inquiry.

GA4 + GTMBehavioral analyticsCustomer evidenceA/B testingService journey

My role

As Growth & Customer Insights Lead, I worked across customer communication, analytics, messaging, and operations. This gave me access to both sides of the journey: what clients were trying to understand and what the team needed to create a viable proposal.

Evidence

I built GA4 and Google Tag Manager tracking for acquisition, landing-page behavior, inquiry flow, and conversion. I paired those signals with recurring questions, consultation conversations, design requests, revisions, and the information clients supplied before moving forward.

Synthesis

The strongest pattern was not a lack of interest. It was uncertainty: clients needed clearer signals about service fit, design scope, priorities, and next steps. Behavioral drop-off made the friction visible; customer language helped explain why it was happening.

Decision

Findings informed clearer service descriptions, landing-page messaging, and inquiry pathways. Current public pages organize wedding services around levels of customization and explicitly ask couples about celebration and design priorities.

02 · Rodosto Software Ltd.

Turning scattered feedback into a shared product signal

In a distributed software environment, evidence lived in different places: user conversations, product feedback, competitor research, search behavior, internal observations, and campaign results. The challenge was to make those signals comparable and useful to several functions.

User interviewsProduct feedbackTriangulationResearch briefsA/B testing

Research question

Where do user expectations and product language diverge, and which signals are strong enough to guide an iteration?

Collaboration

Product · Engineering · Marketing · Distributed operations

My contribution

I supported user interviews and documented recurring needs, questions, and pain points. I organized feedback alongside product observations, competitor patterns, search behavior, and experiment results rather than treating each source as a separate story.

Research operations

Shared documentation preserved current evidence, open questions, and decisions across iterations. Concise briefs gave product, engineering, and marketing collaborators a common reference point.

From insight to action

Audience and messaging hypotheses were compared through structured tests. User language and performance data were used together to refine targeting and copy.

03 · DEMOS Research Collective

Building a defensible cross-case evidence model

Qualitative research becomes difficult when vivid stories compete with patterns across a wider body of material. My work focused on preserving context while creating a consistent way to compare interview evidence, organizational cases, policy sources, and change over time.

Qualitative codingCross-case analysisEvidence matricesLongitudinal researchSensitive data

Analytical principle

A memorable quotation is not automatically a representative finding.

The goal was to maintain a visible chain from raw material to code, pattern, interpretation, and recommendation.

Dataset structure

I worked with a multi-source qualitative dataset spanning interview transcripts, organizational contexts, policy materials, and longitudinal documentary evidence. A consistent framework made it possible to compare cases without flattening meaningful differences.

Analysis

I built comparative evidence matrices around recurring dimensions, examined exceptions and contradictions, and returned repeatedly to source material before advancing a claim. Documentary and policy research helped test whether participant accounts aligned with broader institutional change.

Research integrity

Traceability, anonymization, careful handling of sensitive material, and explicit limitations were central to the work. Findings were written for interdisciplinary audiences while retaining the context needed to understand where a pattern held and where it did not.

Output

The analysis supported research reports and recommendations rather than a simple thematic summary. The resulting structure made complex evidence reviewable by other researchers and stakeholders.

Master’s thesis · Oñati International Institute for the Sociology of Law · 2020

How women build a justice institution they can use

Socio-Legal Imaginations on Women’s Autonomous Organizations: A Case Study on Mala Jinê (Women’s House). I studied how women’s autonomous organizations in North and East Syria developed approaches to gender justice alongside other legal institutions.

Research design

I wrote a 19-question guide in Kurdish and conducted structured remote interviews with representatives connected to women’s organizations. I transcribed Kurdish audio responses, translated them into English, and interpreted them alongside published interviews, news, scholarship, and legal documents, including the region’s Social Contract.

What the evidence showed

The Mala Jin case showed a different route into justice: women could bring problems to a community-based institution that emphasized dialogue, protection, follow-up, and, when needed, referral to other mechanisms. The thesis connected these practices to women’s social and legal autonomy while keeping the political and historical context visible.

Scope: this was an independent qualitative case study with a small primary interview sample and a wider documentary evidence base. Its findings explain this institutional setting; they are not a population estimate.
